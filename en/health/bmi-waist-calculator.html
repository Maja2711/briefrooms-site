General guidance

    How is BMI interpreted?

    For most adults, WHO defines overweight from BMI 25 kg/m² and obesity from 30 kg/m². BMI is a screening measure, not a direct measurement of body fat.

    • <18.5 — underweight
    • 18.5–24.9 — healthy range
    • 25.0–29.9 — overweight
    • 30.0–34.9 — obesity class I
    • 35.0–39.9 — obesity class II
    • ≥40 — obesity class III

    Why waist-to-height ratio?

    NICE recommends using WHtR alongside BMI in adults with BMI below 35 kg/m² as a practical estimate of central adiposity.

    • 0.40–0.49 — healthy central adiposity
    • 0.50–0.59 — increased central adiposity
    • ≥0.60 — high central adiposity

    The simplest message is: keep your waist below half your height.

    Important limitations

    WHtR adds less information when BMI is ≥35 kg/m². BMI should be interpreted cautiously in people with high muscle mass, in older adults and in some ethnic groups where cardiometabolic risk can occur at lower BMI levels.

    This tool is not intended for pregnancy, children or adolescents.
