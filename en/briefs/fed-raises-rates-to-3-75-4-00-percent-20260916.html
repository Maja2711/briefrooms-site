Fed raises rates by 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) on September 16, 2026 raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 3.75–4.00%. The decision was unanimous, 12–0.

Core point

The Fed said inflation remains elevated and that the move supports a timelier return to its 2% inflation goal. It described economic activity as expanding at a solid pace, with resilient domestic spending, strong productivity growth and robust capital investment.

What changes

Effective September 17, the Fed directed operations to maintain the federal funds rate within the 3.75–4.00% range. Interest on reserve balances was set at 3.90%, while the primary credit rate was raised by 25 basis points to 4.00%.

New projections

The September FOMC projections show a median appropriate federal funds rate of 4.1% at the end of 2026, up from 3.8% in the June projection. Median 2026 PCE inflation is projected at 3.7%, with core PCE at 3.4%.

Why it matters

Fed policy affects dollar funding costs, U.S. Treasury valuations, financial conditions and global asset pricing. The shift in the projected policy-rate path is therefore as important for markets as the 25-basis-point increase itself.
