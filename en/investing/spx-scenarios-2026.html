What changed since the 13 Sep House View

1. Growth: a material upward revision

BEA raised Q2 real GDP from 1.5% to 2.2% SAAR. That weakens the case for an imminent recessionary risk-off regime.

2. Consumption: demand remains resilient

Real PCE rose 0.6% m/m in August and nominal PCE rose 0.9%. The consumer is not currently showing a demand collapse.

3. Inflation: still above target

PCE inflation was 3.4% y/y and core PCE 3.0% y/y. That is not low enough to ignore restrictive-rate risk.

4. Fed: an actual hike, not merely hike risk

On 16 September the FOMC raised the target range by 25 bp to 3.75–4.00% and continued to describe inflation as elevated.

5. Long-end yields: the 10Y moved higher

The 10Y Treasury stood at 5.29% on 30 Sep. That raises the equity hurdle rate and restricts multiple expansion.

6. Energy and models: a wider left tail

Brent closed 30 Sep at $115.91. BriefRooms models also show a horizon conflict: the one-month geopolitical signal is defensive, while the shorter-horizon market view does not confirm full risk-off.

Thesis confrontation: resilient growth versus the cost of capital

What supports the market

  • Q2 GDP was revised up to +2.2% SAAR,
  • real PCE +0.6% m/m shows an active consumer,
  • FactSet's analyst bottom-up target of 9,275.04 implied +20.4% versus the 24 Sep close,
  • The short-horizon market-structure model sees a positive S&P 500 setup, although its prospective validation is still immature.

What blocks a higher bias

  • Fed funds at 3.75–4.00% after the September hike,
  • US 10Y at 5.29% raises the discount rate,
  • Brent at $115.91 maintains cost and inflation pressure,
  • The geopolitical model has a defensive S&P 500 signal around the one-month horizon; it remains a research layer and the frozen forecast was outside the 12-hour freshness gate.

Key point: there is no single directional signal today. Growth data are better than on 13 Sep, but capital costs and energy are also higher. That leaves 1M neutral-defensive, 6M neutral and 12M mildly positive on a conditional basis.

What changes House View

We raise the bias if

  • Brent sustainably falls below ~$90,
  • the 10Y falls below ~4.7% without a recession signal,
  • PCE/core PCE cool materially and the Fed no longer needs to hike,
  • positive EPS revisions broaden beyond megacap/AI,
  • the one-month geopolitical signal stops being defensive for the S&P 500.

We lower the bias if

  • Brent remains above ~$110 for additional weeks,
  • the 10Y establishes itself above ~5.2%,
  • the Fed signals further hikes while real demand weakens,
  • EPS/margin revisions turn broadly negative,
  • BriefRooms models begin to confirm the same defensive medium-horizon regime.
